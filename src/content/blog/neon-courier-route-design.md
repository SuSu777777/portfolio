**Neon Courier is a fictional Unity game concept**, not a completed game. The player carries packages through a compact night city. The interesting choice is not just how fast to move, but which route to take.

## Give every route a tradeoff

A straight road might be easy to read but crowded. A rooftop shortcut might save time but require a precise jump. A tunnel might be safer until it closes at a certain hour. If one route is always best, the map stops being a game.

I would build a first district with three delivery points and three clearly different paths. The player should be able to make a plan from the map, then adapt when the city changes.

## Make speed feel earned

Movement should begin with a small set of dependable actions: accelerate, brake, turn, and boost. Good handling gives players a reason to improve. A boost meter could refill when they take a clean corner or chain deliveries efficiently, rewarding skill without forcing constant speed.

Visual cues should support fast decisions. Delivery destinations need a recognizable color, shortcuts need a consistent shape, and hazards must be visible early enough to react.

## Test the city, not its size

Before adding more streets, I would ask a player to complete the same three deliveries several times. Do they try a different route? Do they remember a shortcut? Can they tell why they were faster? If the answer is yes, the small district already has replay value.

That is a stronger foundation than a large city with no meaningful choices.
